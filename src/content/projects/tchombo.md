## The pitch

"How far can you push it?" is the whole game in one line. Each round drops a real fact about Mauritius, a date, a distance, a population, and players take turns naming a number higher than the last, until someone calls TCHOMBO on the player before them (betting they've already overshot), or calls it exact and risks everything on one guess. No install, no account: a four-digit room code and fifteen dodos' worth of nerve.

## Built the same way it plays: out loud

The real-time engine, the bilingual interface, the trivia itself: all of it came out of a running conversation with an AI pair programmer instead of a blank editor, over a few weeks of back and forth. What came out the other side is a proper shared game-state server, 860 fact-checked questions about the island in English and French, and a small flock of mascot dodos: one smug, one mid-celebration, one very visibly regretting its life choices.

## What it actually rewards

The trivia is the vehicle, not the point. The real game is reading the room: how far the last player will push a number before someone calls their bluff. Guess wrong and you lose dodos, and missing an easy one costs more than missing a brutal one, the one rule that keeps everybody honest.
